# Anti-Patterns — Never Do These

Check every email against this list before delivering. A brand guide can override individual items only if the brand explicitly and demonstrably uses that pattern. If a brief asks for something on this list, flag it before building.

---

**Multi-column body layouts beyond 2-up product grids.**
Single column is the rule. 2×2 product grids and 2-column comparison tables are the only exceptions.

**More than one primary CTA color per email.**
Pick the brand's primary CTA color and use it consistently. A second color creates visual ambiguity about which action matters most.

**Two competing CTAs in the hero.**
The hero gets one button. If the brief includes two hero CTAs, flag it to Prescott — it means the email has two messages, which means two emails.

**Gradient background panels behind body text.**
Gradients reduce legibility on text-heavy panels. Solid colors only for sections containing copy. Gradients are acceptable on purely decorative or image-adjacent elements with no text on top.

**Decorative SVG illustrations — flowers, sparkles, abstract shapes, ornamental dividers.**
Unless the brand's existing email or site design explicitly uses them. The exception is functional SVG (icon-led list markers, wave dividers that serve as section transitions).

**Drop shadows on text.**
Never apply `text-shadow` to email copy. It reduces legibility and is stripped or broken by many email clients. Contrast and overlay opacity are the correct tools.

**Boxes nested inside boxes inside boxes.**
One level of card on one band is the maximum. A card may sit on a section band. A card may not contain another card. Nested boxing signals unclear hierarchy — flatten it.

**Stock photos of "happy people holding a phone"** or any generic lifestyle photography with no relationship to the brand or product. Every image must earn its place. If a real client photo doesn't exist for a section, use a texture, a color block, or leave the space — do not fill it with stock.

**Three or more typefaces.**
Maximum of two font families per email. Never introduce a third.

**Tiny text — body below 14px, footer/legal below 11px.**
If a design is pushing copy below these floors to make something "fit," the section is overcrowded and the copy needs to be cut, not shrunk.

**Filler social proof without a real quote and real attribution.**
No placeholder testimonials, no vague star displays, no anonymous reviews. Real name + real quote, OR a single hard number with a source. If the brief has no social proof to include, omit the section.

**"Lorem ipsum" or any placeholder copy left in a deliverable.**
Write tight placeholder copy that reads as if it could ship. Flag every placeholder clearly with brackets. Never leave lorem ipsum. Never leave a blank section.

**Inventing prices, claims, ingredients, or product names.**
If a price is not confirmed: `[PRICE TBC]`. If a product name is not confirmed: `[PRODUCT NAME TBC]`. Fabricated specifics in an email can ship to thousands of subscribers — zero tolerance.
