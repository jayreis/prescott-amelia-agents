# CPG DTC Email Design Research
## What top consumer brands do visually — and why it works

Last updated: 2026-05-15
Applies to: all CPG/DTC clients

---

## 1. Photography Vocabulary

CPG DTC brands operate from a defined set of shot types. Amelia must know which type she is reaching for before selecting from image_library.json.

**Hero / Packshot**
Clean product against white or branded background. All angles (front, back, angled). Prioritizes packaging legibility — label is readable, color is accurate. Used in product spotlight sections and product grids.

**Lifestyle**
Product in use in a real environment. Real person, real context, real moment. For food/beverage this means in-kitchen, at-gym, out-of-bag. Not studio-clinical — it should feel documentary, not posed. This is the primary hero image type for wellness and CPG brands that want to feel human.

**Ingredient / Flavor Cue**
Ingredients arranged around or spilling from the product. Communicates taste, function, or formula at a glance. Works as a hero or secondary section anchor. Example: a scoop of protein with whey, berries, and oats arranged beneath it.

**Flat Lay / Abundance**
Overhead arrangement. Product centered, ingredients or lifestyle props spread around it. Creates a "variety and richness" signal. Works well for food, supplement, and personal care CPG.

**Cut-Out / Float**
Background removed. Product floats within a color-blocked section. Creates depth and editorial quality — reads closer to magazine than catalog. Overlapping section edges is permitted and often looks premium.

**Motion / Action**
Pours, splashes, spins. Dynamic photography outperforms static in CPG. Where available, use the most dynamic image in the hero. Where motion video exists, favor it over static.

**In-Hand / Usage**
Product held by a hand, held near a face, or shown being consumed. Gives scale and context. Builds the sensory connection that a clean packshot cannot.

## 2. Section Layout Patterns

### Z-Pattern (default for promo and campaign emails)
Eye travels: top-left → top-right → diagonal down → bottom CTA.
Use for: single-offer promo, product launch, flash sale.
Key rule: one hero image dominates top half; CTA at bottom-right of the Z-path.

### F-Pattern (education, newsletter, multi-section)
Eye travels: horizontal scan at top → vertical down left edge → horizontal glances.
Use for: ingredient education, welcome series, loyalty/rewards.
Key rule: important subheads and CTAs anchor the left edge.

### Single Column / Color Band Rhythm (most CPG emails)
Alternating color bands create visual separation without multi-column risk. Each band is one section. Light → dark → light → dark. Product or image in each band — never a copy-only band.

## 3. Hero Section Design

Top CPG brands treat the hero as a full creative unit, not just a banner.

**What it must contain:**
- Full-bleed background image (lifestyle or ingredient photography)
- Brand color overlay at minimum 0.60 opacity if text sits on top
- Product integrated into the image (not just a photo behind the headline)
- Eyebrow label (e.g. "NEW" / "LIMITED BATCH" / "THIS WEEK ONLY") — optional but effective for urgency
- Headline: benefit-led or offer-led, never brand-led
- Subhead: adds the "why now" or the supporting proof point
- CTA: one button, above the fold, always

**What top brands do differently:**
- OLIPOP uses the hero to let "the main story take center stage" — never cluttered
- AG1 structures the hero around one clear benefit claim backed by a visual of the product
- Magic Spoon injects brand personality directly into the hero color and type choices
- All high-performing CPG heroes show the product clearly — subscriber must be able to identify the SKU at a glance

**What to avoid in the hero:**
- Two competing messages
- Founder copy or brand backstory ("A note from our team...")
- Unmasked text over photograph — always apply overlay first
- Beauty-over-clarity: visually striking but the offer is unclear

## 4. CPG-Specific Section Types

These section patterns appear consistently across high-performing CPG DTC email programs. Amelia should recognize which section type each brief calls for and select the right visual approach accordingly.

### Benefit Callout Row
3–4 benefits in icon-led or numbered row format. Icons are functional SVG (not decorative). Copy is 1 sentence per benefit. Each benefit states a specific outcome — not a feature. Section background: dark or brand-accent band.

### Ingredient Transparency Block
Ingredient shot (flat lay or ingredient cue) as the section background or section image. Brief copy explaining what's in the product and why it matters. Effective for supplement, food, and beverage CPG. Signals trust — this brand is not hiding what's inside.

### Science / Proof Block
A clinical stat, study result, or third-party validation. Visual: hard number in large type OR a credentialed expert photo (doctor, nutritionist, athlete). Copy: one sentence of context. Do not write this section without a confirmed source from the brand.

### Lifestyle Integration Section
Full-bleed lifestyle photo. Product in use. Brief copy (2 sentences max) that places the subscriber inside the moment. Often paired with a single customer quote (real name, real words). This section converts when it makes the subscriber feel the product rather than understand it.

### Flavor / Variant Grid
2×2 or 3×1 product grid showing multiple SKUs. Clean packshots on white or brand color. Used for new product launches, subscription upsell ("try all four"), or seasonal collection drops. This is the one exception to single-column default.

### UGC / Social Proof Block
One real customer photo (if available) OR one real quote with full name and star rating. Supported by a hard aggregate number ("12,000+ reviews, 4.8 stars"). Never a wall of stars. Never anonymous. Never fabricated. If no real social proof exists, omit the section.

## 5. Photography Treatment in Email

### Lifestyle shot with overlay
Dark overlay before any text. Minimum rgba(0,0,0,0.60). Increase to 0.75+ for lighter photos. White text on top. Never unmasked.

### Cut-out product on color block
Remove background. Product floats. Place on a solid color band matching the brand palette. More editorial than a plain packshot. Allows product to overlap section borders — encouraged when it reads well.

### Ingredient pour / abundance as hero
The ingredient arrangement IS the visual. Product centered. Ingredients radiate outward. No overlay needed if the section has a solid or colored background. Copy placed on adjacent color block, not on top of the ingredients image.

### Variant grid on white or brand color
All SKUs photographed from the same angle and height. Consistent lighting. When image_library.json has inconsistent product shots, note it and use only the ones that match — never mix studio styles in the same grid.

### In-hand / usage shot in secondary section
Placed in the lifestyle integration section or below the hero. Not in the hero if the lifestyle photo is stronger. Always paired with benefit copy — the image alone is not enough.

## 6. Color Blocking

**What it is:** Each section of the email has its own background color — alternating between dark, light, and brand-accent bands. The reader experiences the email as a sequence of distinct visual environments.

**Why it works:** Prevents the "endless white page" problem. Creates natural section breaks without relying on hard borders or horizontal rules. Each band feels like a designed decision, not a default.

**CPG-specific rules:**
- Magic Spoon uses vibrant, product-color-matched bands — each section can feel like a different flavor
- OLIPOP uses clean white space as a breathing section between color moments
- AG1 uses a controlled palette (dark green, cream, white) — the color blocking is subtle but consistent
- Always follow the client's own brand.md for color hierarchy

**How to apply:**
- Alternate dark and light bands throughout the email
- Never use gradient backgrounds behind body copy
- Brand accent color reserved for CTA sections and highlight moments — not large section backgrounds (per Amelia's existing rule)
- If the brand has multiple product colors (flavors, variants), the section for that product can pull its color

## 7. Typography Patterns in Top CPG Emails

### Benefit-led hierarchy
**Benefit (what it does)** > **Product name** > **Brand name**
The subscriber reads the outcome first. The product is the vehicle. The brand is the context.

### Size and weight
Headlines run large — 28–36px in email context. Section subheads 20–24px. Body 14–16px (already in Amelia's rules). The gap between headline and body is wide — this is intentional. It makes the email scannable in 3 seconds.

### Short headline structures common in CPG
- Statement of outcome: "Recovery that actually works."
- Question to the subscriber: "Still sleeping through alarms?"
- Offer-anchored: "30 days free. No commitment."
- Number-led: "75 nutrients. One scoop."

These are punchy, declarative, often sentence fragments. The pattern matches Amelia's existing copy rules — reinforce them here.

## 8. Social Proof Placement

**AG1 approach:** Expert or influencer testimonial placed directly after the benefit callout section. The expert's photo + name + credential. One quote. Then CTA. This creates a "reason to believe" moment at the decision point.

**OLIPOP approach:** Customer review that echoes the main selling point, placed near the closing CTA. Converts best when the review language mirrors the email's offer language.

**Magic Spoon approach:** Brand personality carries more weight than social proof in their emails — they lean on fun visual design over testimonials.

**Universal rules (already in Amelia's standards, reinforced here):**
- Real name, real quote, or hard aggregate number
- Never anonymous
- Never fabricated
- One social proof block per email max
- If no real proof exists, omit entirely

## 9. CTA Design Notes

**Color contrast is critical for CPG brands.** AG1's green CTAs get lost against their green brand palette. Always verify the CTA button color is meaningfully distinct from the section background. If the brand's primary color is also the dominant section color, use white or a neutral for the button background and reverse the text.

**Arrow directionality** in CTAs increases click-through in CPG emails. A right-facing arrow after the CTA label signals "forward motion" and is effective for fitness, wellness, and performance brands.

**OLIPOP's tiered CTA system:**
- Primary: full button → conversion (personalized cart or PDP)
- Secondary: unobtrusive banner or footer link → re-engagement or re-order
Do not apply this two-tier system unless the brief explicitly calls for it — single CTA is the default.

## 10. What Separates Top CPG Email Design from Average

| Pattern | Average | Top CPG |
|---|---|---|
| Hero photography | Stock lifestyle, loose connection to brand | Brand-owned or brand-directed, product visible |
| Section rhythm | Alternating copy and image | Color band rhythm, each section has a visual anchor |
| Product visibility | Product appears once in hero | Product visible in 2-3 sections at different scales |
| Benefit communication | Paragraph copy | Icon-row or number-led, 1 sentence per benefit |
| Social proof | Star ratings, vague quotes | Named expert or customer + specific claim |
| CTA | One button at the bottom | Repeated 2-3x, label evolves, same destination |
| Copy length | 3-4 paragraphs | 2 sentences per section max, scanned in 3 seconds |
| Color | White background throughout | Color-banded, brand palette consistent top to bottom |
